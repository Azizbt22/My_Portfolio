01 — About

Profile

I'm a newly graduated engineer from Mines Saint-Étienne, specialising in data science and AI. I came to this field through mathematics and a broad engineering education, and I've found that I enjoy both the theory and the work of putting an idea into practice.

I'm curious by nature. When I come across an unfamiliar problem, I like asking questions, understanding the context and working through possible approaches. My projects have let me explore machine learning, language models, data analysis and software development. They've also taught me to look closely at results, especially when something doesn't work as expected.

I'm looking for my first role in AI engineering, data science or technology consulting. I'd like to keep improving technically, discover new domains and work with people who are happy to share what they know. I want to take on challenging problems and grow into someone who can see them through from the initial need to a sound implementation.

What interests me most

Understanding the problem before choosing an approach. Finding where data or AI can make a useful difference. Then figuring out how to implement it clearly and efficiently, with the constraints in mind.

02 — Journey

Where I learned, worked and grew.

A path through broad engineering, international study and my first professional experiences.

Tunisia · 2019–2021

Building the mathematical foundation

At IPEST, the rhythm of the preparatory programme taught me how to work through difficult material under pressure. Mathematics and physics became a way to decompose a problem, test an argument and keep going when the answer was not immediate.

  • Intensive mathematics and physics
  • Competitive-exam preparation
  • A demanding collective environment

Saint-Étienne · 2021–2026

Learning to think across disciplines

Mines Saint-Étienne is a general engineering school. I moved between computer science, applied mathematics, physics, project management and finance before choosing to specialise in data and AI. At the same time, I completed a mathematics licence at Université Jean Monnet. That breadth taught me to see a problem from more than one angle.

  • General engineering across science, software and management
  • Mathematics licence at Université Jean Monnet
  • Specialisation in AI and data science

Germany · 2024–2025

Finding my feet abroad

My Erasmus semester at TU Dresden was about much more than classes. I arrived in a new country, found friends from around the world and learned to feel at home in a different culture. I also studied machine learning, computer vision and language models. Working with people who brought different perspectives made the experience especially memorable.

  • International friendships and a new academic culture
  • More independence and confidence in unfamiliar settings
  • Machine learning, computer vision and LLM coursework

Paris · Summer 2025

My first professional experience in data

My internship at the HEGP clinical research unit was my first opportunity to work on data questions in a professional setting. I learned to take the time to understand a researcher's question, discuss results carefully and be honest about what an analysis can show. Working alongside people whose expertise was different from mine confirmed that this is the field I want to pursue.

  • Working with researchers and learning their perspective
  • Clear communication and careful interpretation
  • A stronger conviction that I wanted to work in data

Évry · 2025–2026

Broadening my data perspective

At Télécom SudParis, I expanded my knowledge through courses in machine learning, data visualisation, deep learning and LLM architecture. The people I worked with came from different countries, disciplines and career stages; some had already worked in industry. Those team projects taught me as much about exchanging ideas as they did about the subjects themselves.

  • ML, deep learning, data visualisation and LLM architecture
  • Applied projects across data and AI
  • Multicultural, multidisciplinary teamwork

Paris · 2026

Taking an idea all the way through

My final internship at Devoteam gave me ownership of a project from the initial need to a working tool. I learned to make choices, explain them to others, listen to feedback and keep improving what I built. It was a shift from completing a technical exercise to thinking about how a solution would actually be used.

  • Taking responsibility for an end-to-end project
  • Explaining decisions and responding to feedback
  • Connecting technical work with practical use
